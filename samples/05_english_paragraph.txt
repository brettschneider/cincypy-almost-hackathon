I was reminded this morning of a conversation I had with my grandmother
when I was about ten years old. She was showing me how to make her
potato soup, the same recipe her mother had taught her, which was
itself adapted from a version brought over from the old country in
1923. The trick, she said, was not in the ingredients but in the
patience. You have to let the onions sweat for a long time before
anything else goes in. You have to taste the broth three or four
times during the cooking, and adjust. Most people, she said, rush the
broth. They think the recipe is a list of instructions. But the recipe
is really a way of paying attention to what is in front of you.

I have been thinking about that a lot lately, in contexts that have
nothing to do with soup. It seems to me that most of what goes wrong
in software, in writing, in relationships, in the news, comes from
people not willing to spend the time to let the onions sweat. We want
the answer before the question has finished forming. We want the fix
before we have understood the problem. I do not have a tidy conclusion
to offer here. I just thought it was worth saying.
